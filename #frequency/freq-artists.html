The artists who shaped the sound of generations

Papa Roach

Papa Roach formed in 1993 in California, USA. They became widely known for their hit song "Last Resort", which brought them international success. One of their greatest achievements was the worldwide success of their album Infest, which was certified multi-platinum. Their contribution to music was helping bring nu metal and alternative rock to a much wider mainstream audience in the early 2000s.

The Police

The Police formed in 1977 in London, England. They became internationally famous for songs such as "Every Breath You Take", which remains one of their most recognized songs. One of their greatest achievements was winning five Grammy Awards and becoming one of the most successful British bands of the late 1970s and 1980s. Their contribution to music was their distinctive blend of rock, reggae, punk, and new wave.

Bon Jovi

Bon Jovi formed in 1983 in New Jersey, USA. They became internationally famous for songs such as "Livin' on a Prayer" and "You Give Love a Bad Name." One of their greatest achievements was being inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2018. Their contribution to music was helping define arena rock and mainstream hard rock during the 1980s and beyond.

Queen

Queen formed in 1970 in London, England. They became internationally famous for songs such as “Bohemian Rhapsody” and “We Will Rock You.” One of their greatest achievements was being inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2001. Their contribution to music was their unique combination of rock, opera, theatrical performance, and innovative songwriting, which influenced generations of musicians.

Metallica

Metallica formed in 1981 in Los Angeles, USA. They became internationally famous for songs such as “Enter Sandman” and “Master of Puppets.” One of their greatest achievements was being inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2009. Their contribution to music was helping pioneer and popularize thrash metal, combining speed, heavy guitar riffs, and complex songwriting.

Nirvana

Nirvana formed in 1987 in Aberdeen, Washington, USA. They became internationally famous for songs such as “Smells Like Teen Spirit” and “Come as You Are.” One of their greatest achievements was the enormous success of their album Nevermind, which helped bring grunge into the mainstream. Their contribution to music was helping define the grunge and alternative rock movement of the early 1990s.

Guns N' Roses

Guns N' Roses formed in 1985 in Los Angeles, USA. They became internationally famous for songs such as “Sweet Child o' Mine” and “Welcome to the Jungle.” One of their greatest achievements was the massive success of their debut album Appetite for Destruction, one of the best-selling debut albums ever. Their contribution to music was helping revive hard rock and guitar-driven rock music in the late 1980s.

Tame Impala

Tame Impala, the psychedelic music project led by Kevin Parker, began in 2007 and became known for its dreamy, experimental sound blending psychedelic rock, pop, and electronic music. It is famous for songs such as “The Less I Know the Better” and “Feels Like We Only Go Backwards.” One of its greatest achievements was winning the Grammy Award for Best Dance/Electronic Recording for “Neverender” in 2025, further establishing Tame Impala as one of the most influential modern psychedelic music projects.

Arctic Monkeys

Arctic Monkeys are an English rock band formed in 2002 in Sheffield, known for their energetic sound and evolution from indie rock to more experimental styles. They are famous for songs such as “Do I Wanna Know?” and “505.” One of their greatest achievements is winning seven Brit Awards, including three wins for Best British Group, and becoming one of the most successful British rock bands of their generation.

Linkin Park

Linkin Park formed in 1996 in California, USA. They became internationally famous for songs such as “In the End” and “Numb.” One of their greatest achievements was winning two Grammy Awards and selling millions of albums worldwide. Their contribution to music was their influential blend of rock, metal, hip-hop, and electronic music, which helped shape the sound of 2000s alternative rock.

Michael Jackson

Michael Jackson began his professional career as a member of the Jackson 5 in 1964 and later launched his solo career. He became internationally famous for songs such as “Billie Jean” and “Beat It.” One of his greatest achievements was Thriller, the best-selling album of all time. His contribution to music extended beyond singing, as he revolutionized pop performance, dance, music videos, and live entertainment. Michael Jackson became one of the most influential figures in popular music. His singing, dancing and innovative music videos helped transform pop music and its visual presentation around the world.

Indian Musical Legends

Shankar Mahadevan

Shankar Mahadevan , often known as the “Voice of Versatility,” began his professional music career in the 1990s and became one of India’s most celebrated playback singers and composers. He is famous for songs like “Breathless,” which became iconic for its unique rapid vocal style. One of his greatest achievements is winning multiple National Film Awards and Filmfare Awards , along with receiving the prestigious Padma Shri for his contribution to Indian music.

A. R. Rahman

A. R. Rahman — “Isai Puyal” A. R. Rahman began his professional career as a composer in the early 1990s and rose to fame through Tamil and Hindi cinema. He is famous for songs such as “Jai Ho” and “Vande Mataram.” One of his greatest achievements was winning two Academy Awards for Slumdog Millionaire. His contribution to music includes blending Indian classical music with electronic, orchestral, and global sounds.

Ilaiyaraaja

Ilaiyaraaja , known as “Isaignani” (Musical Genius), began his professional music career in the 1970s and became one of India’s most influential composers. He is famous for songs such as “Rakkamma Kaiya Thattu” and has composed music for thousands of films across Indian cinema. One of his greatest achievements is winning five National Film Awards and receiving the prestigious Padma Vibhushan for his immense contribution to Indian music.

Kishore Kumar

Kishore Kumar , known as the “King of Melody,” began his playback singing career in the 1940s and became one of the most iconic voices in Indian cinema. He is famous for songs such as “Roop Tera Mastana” and “Mere Sapno Ki Rani,” which remain classics of Hindi music. One of his greatest achievements was winning eight Filmfare Awards for Best Male Playback Singer , the highest number at the time, and leaving a lasting legacy as a singer, actor, composer, and filmmaker.

Lata Mangeshkar

Lata Mangeshkar , known as the “Nightingale of India,” began her professional singing career in the 1940s and became one of the most influential playback singers in Indian music. She is famous for timeless songs such as “Lag Ja Gale” and “Ajeeb Dastan Hai Yeh.” One of her greatest achievements was receiving the Bharat Ratna , India’s highest civilian award, along with numerous National Film Awards and Filmfare Awards for her extraordinary contribution to music.

Mohammed Rafi

Mohammed Rafi , known as the “Voice of the Millennium,” began his professional singing career in the 1940s and became one of the most celebrated playback singers in Indian cinema. He is famous for timeless songs such as “Chura Liya Hai Tumne Jo Dil Ko” and “Baharon Phool Barsao.” One of his greatest achievements was winning six National Film Awards and six Filmfare Awards , along with receiving the prestigious Padma Shri for his contribution to Indian music.

Asha Bhosle

Asha Bhosle , known as the “Queen of Playback Singing,” began her professional singing career in the 1940s and became one of the most versatile and celebrated voices in Indian music. She is famous for songs such as “Dum Maro Dum” and “Piya Tu Ab To Aaja.” One of her greatest achievements was winning two National Film Awards and seven Filmfare Awards, and she was honoured with the Padma Vibhushan for her outstanding contribution to Indian music.

S. P. Balasubrahmanyam

S. P. Balasubrahmanyam (SPB) , known as “Paadum Nila” (Singing Moon), began his professional singing career in the 1960s and became one of India’s most prolific playback singers. He is famous for songs such as “Tere Mere Beech Mein” and “Mannil Indha Kadhal.” One of his greatest achievements was winning six National Film Awards for Best Male Playback Singer and receiving the prestigious Padma Vibhushan for his immense contribution to Indian music.

K. J. Yesudas

K. J. Yesudas , known as “Gana Gandharvan” (Celestial Singer), began his professional singing career in the 1960s and became one of India’s most respected playback singers. He is famous for songs such as “Harivarasanam” and “Gori Tera Gaon Bada Pyara.” One of his greatest achievements was winning eight National Film Awards , the highest number by an Indian male playback singer, and receiving the prestigious Padma Vibhushan for his extraordinary contribution to Indian music.

Ravi Shankar

Ravi Shankar , known as the “Sitar Maestro,” began performing professionally in the 1930s and became one of the most influential Indian classical musicians in the world. He is famous for his exceptional sitar performances and for popularizing Indian classical music internationally. One of his greatest achievements was winning three Grammy Awards and receiving the Bharat Ratna , India’s highest civilian honour, for his extraordinary contribution to music.

M. S. Subbulakshmi

M. S. Subbulakshmi, known as the “Queen of Carnatic Music,”began performing publicly at a young age and went on to become one of India’s most revered classical singers. She is famous for devotional songs such as “Kurai Ondrum Illai” and her renditions of Carnatic compositions. One of her greatest achievements was becoming the first musician to receive the Bharat Ratna , India’s highest civilian honour, in recognition of her immense contribution to Indian classical music.

Ustad Bismillah Khan

Ustad Bismillah Khan, known as the “Shehnai Maestro,”began performing professionally in the 1930s and became one of the greatest shehnai players in Indian classical music. He is famous for popularizing the shehnai as a concert instrument and for his iconic performances at major national events. One of his greatest achievements was receiving the Bharat Ratna, India’s highest civilian honour, in recognition of his extraordinary contribution to Indian music.

Atif Aslam

Atif Aslam , known as the "Voice of Romance,” began his professional music career in the early 2000s as the lead vocalist of the band Jal before launching his successful solo career. He is famous for songs such as “Aadat”and “Tera Hone Laga Hoon.” One of his greatest achievements is becoming one of the most popular Pakistani playback singers internationally, with numerous hit songs across Pakistani and Indian films.

Arijit Singh

Arijit Singh, known as the “Melody King,” began his professional singing career in the late 2000s and became one of the most popular playback singers in Indian cinema. He is famous for songs such as “Tum Hi Ho” and “Channa Mereya.” One of his greatest achievements is winning multiple Filmfare Awards and National Film Awards, as well as receiving the prestigious Padma Shri for his contribution to Indian music.

Shreya Ghoshal

Shreya Ghoshal, known as the “Melody Queen of India,” began her playback singing career in the early 2000s and became one of the most celebrated voices in Indian cinema. She is famous for songs such as “Dola Re Dola” and “Teri Ore.” One of her greatest achievements is winning six National Film Awards and numerous Filmfare Awards, along with receiving the Padma Shri for her contribution to Indian music.
